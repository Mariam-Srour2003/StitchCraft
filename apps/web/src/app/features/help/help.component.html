Getting started

  1. Create a project on the Projects page. A project groups related patterns, like "Christmas gifts".
  2. Start a pattern: blank, from a template, or by converting a photo.
  3. Draw with Paint, Fill, shapes and lettering. Add DMC threads from the palette search.
  4. Check materials: the legend shows stitches and skeins per color. Add what you own to My threads, then see what to buy on the Shopping list.
  5. Export a printable PDF chart, or open the pattern view and print it.
  6. Stitch: switch the editor to Stitch mode and tick off squares as you go.

Crafts

Cross-stitch

Each square on the chart is one X on the fabric. Work one color at a time, finishing a row of half stitches before crossing back. Use Highlight in the editor or legend to see where a color goes.

Diamond painting

Each square is one drill. Peel back a small part of the film, pour the drills for one color in a tray, and press them onto the matching symbols. Use the Diamond view to preview the look.

Paint by number

The Paint # view outlines each region and numbers it once. Paint the smallest regions and lightest colors first. Mark regions as painted in Stitch mode to see your progress fill in.

Backstitch

Backstitch lines outline shapes and add fine detail like lettering, whiskers or stems. In the editor pick Backstitch (or press J), choose a thread color, and drag from one grid corner to another. Lines snap to corners and can run along cell edges or diagonally. Drawing over an existing line in a new color recolors it; Remove line deletes the line you click. Backstitch thread is added to that color's skein count, and lines appear on the printed chart.

Half and quarter stitches

Partial stitches smooth curves and add detail smaller than a square (cross-stitch patterns only). Pick Half (D) and click near a corner of a square: the stitch runs along the diagonal through that corner. Pick Quarter (Q) and click a quarter of a square: the stitch runs from that corner to the centre. A half plus a quarter on the other diagonal makes a three-quarter stitch, and up to four quarters in different colors can share a square. Clicking the same stitch again removes it; painting or erasing the square replaces them. Their thread is counted in the skeins (a half stitch as half a cross, a quarter as a quarter), and they appear on the printed chart.

Converting photos

In a project, choose Convert image. Before converting you can rotate, flip, crop (free or a fixed shape), and adjust brightness, contrast and saturation. The photo check looks at the photo with your settings and tells you how well it will stitch: whether faces will be big enough to recognise (aim for about 30 stitches tall - people far away in a photo turn into a blur), whether the photo is blurry, dark, flat or stretched, and whether busy detail will scatter into single stitches. Each finding has a one-click fix, and Pattern preview shows the result in real thread colors before you convert.

Auto-enhance stretches the contrast and lifts colors (threads look duller than a screen). Clean up recolors lone stitches to match their neighbours, folds colors used for only a few stitches into the closest one, and for paint by number merges areas too small to paint. The result opens in the editor, where you can paint, erase, fill, add and delete colors, and use Tidy for more clean-up.

Stitching mode

In the editor, switch from Design to Stitch. Clicking or dragging marks squares done (drag across already-done squares to un-mark them). "Mark done" finishes a whole color at once. Progress is saved with the pattern when you press Save.
